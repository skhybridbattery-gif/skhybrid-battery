C1391 Code on Toyota Prius & Aqua: What It Means and How to Fix It

ABS, VSC and brake lights all came on together, and the scanner shows C1391? This is one of the most common brake faults on Toyota hybrids in Pakistan. Here is what the code means, whether the car is safe to drive, why clearing it does not work, and what a proper fix costs.

What Does C1391 Mean?

Toyota describes C1391 as an abnormal leak in the accumulator. In plain terms, the brake actuator cannot hold hydraulic pressure the way it should.

Toyota hybrids use electronically controlled brakes. An electric pump charges an accumulator, a small pressure store, with brake fluid. The skid control computer uses that stored pressure to apply the brakes, run ABS and VSC, and blend hydraulic braking with regenerative braking from the motor.

After the pump stops, the pressure should hold steady. If it drops faster than expected, the computer concludes that fluid is leaking past a seal or valve inside the unit and stores C1391. The leak is internal, so you will not usually see fluid on the ground.

Which cars get C1391?

C1391 appears on Toyota and Lexus hybrids that use this accumulator-based brake system. In Pakistan we see it most on:

  • Toyota Prius Gen 2 and Gen 3
  • Toyota Aqua
  • Corolla Axio and Fielder Hybrid
  • Lexus CT200h
  • Camry Hybrid

The layout differs between models. On some, the pump and accumulator are a separate assembly from the valve unit; on others they are combined. The meaning of the code is the same, but the part that needs replacing is not, which is why the model and year matter for the quote.

Symptoms

Symptoms That Come With C1391

You may notice one or several of these before, or at the same time as, the warning lights.

ABS, VSC and Brake Lights

The ABS light, the VSC or skid light and the red brake warning come on together, often with a master warning or "Check VSC System" message.

Pump Runs Too Often

A healthy pump buzzes for a few seconds when you open the door or press the pedal. With a leak it runs again and again, even while the car is parked.

Hard Brake Pedal

When the system falls back to backup braking, the pedal feels firm and needs more effort, and the car takes longer to stop. Treat this as a safety warning.

Less Regenerative Braking

The system reduces or switches off regeneration. The hybrid battery charges less while braking and the fuel average drops.

What Causes C1391?

  • Worn seals and valves inside the actuator. This is the most common cause. Once fluid leaks internally, the unit cannot hold pressure.
  • A tired accumulator. With age the accumulator stores less pressure, so the pump has to run more often.
  • Old, contaminated brake fluid. Fluid that has absorbed moisture corrodes and wears the fine valves and seals from inside.
  • A weak pump motor. A pump that cannot build pressure quickly can trigger C1391 together with pump-related codes.
Why it is so common in Pakistan: most hybrids here are imported from Japan at 5 to 10 years old, often with the brake fluid never changed. DOT 3 fluid absorbs moisture from the air over time, and moist fluid in an actuator that sits in 40°C+ heat for months each year wears out seals much faster. Many of the Aqua and Prius actuators we replace in Karachi have dark, old fluid inside.

Can You Drive With C1391?

In most cases the car will still stop, because the system has a hydraulic backup. But this is your brakes, so be careful.

  • Short, gentle trips only. Drive slowly to your home or a nearby workshop, keep extra distance and avoid highways and long journeys.
  • Expect no ABS or VSC. On a wet or sandy road the wheels can lock under hard braking.
  • Hard pedal means stop. If the pedal suddenly goes hard, the car pulls to one side, or the fluid level is below the minimum mark, park safely and call for help.

How to Fix C1391

Actuator replacement vs repair

Because C1391 points to an internal leak, the usual fix is replacing the brake actuator, or on some models the pump and accumulator assembly, with a tested unit. Repair is possible where the scan shows an electrical fault, such as a pump relay or module problem, rather than a leak. A resealed leaking unit rarely lasts in our heat.

Why a cleared code comes back

Many owners are told the code was "cleared" and the lights went off, only for them to return a day or a week later. Clearing a code erases memory; it does not stop a leak. As soon as the computer sees pressure dropping again, it sets C1391 once more.

Electronic bleeding and calibration

These brakes cannot be bled like an ordinary car. After the actuator is replaced, a scan tool runs the bleeding routine to purge air from the actuator and accumulator. The technician then runs the zero-point calibrations the brake computer needs.

If calibration is skipped, the lights stay on and C1345 (linear solenoid offset not learned) is stored. That is a sign the job was not finished, not a new fault.

How to Prevent C1391

  • Flush the brake fluid every 3 years or 40,000 km, whichever comes first, using the scan-tool procedure. It is the cheapest protection for an expensive actuator.
  • Change the fluid soon after buying an imported hybrid, unless you have proof it was done recently.
  • Use the correct fresh fluid and do not mix brands or top up from an old, opened bottle.
  • Listen to the pump. If it buzzes more often than before, get a scan before the warning lights come on.
