Symptoms

Signs Your Aqua Battery Is Failing

An Aqua pack rarely dies overnight. It fades over a few months, and the car tells you in these ways. If you notice two or more, book a free diagnostic.

⛽

Fuel Average Drops

A healthy Aqua gives 25–30 km/L. When the pack weakens the engine does all the work and the average falls to 12–15 km/L, close to a normal petrol car.

🔋

Battery Gauge Jumps Full to Empty

The gauge shows full, drops to one or two bars within a minute of driving, then climbs back to full while braking. Weak modules cannot hold a charge, so the reading swings.

⚠️

Hybrid System Warning / Red Triangle

"Check Hybrid System" with the red triangle and master warning light. The car may go into limp mode or refuse to start. The pack has failed a self-test.

🔊

Engine Runs Constantly at Idle

A healthy Aqua switches the engine off at signals. If it keeps running or restarts every 30 seconds, the pack cannot hold enough charge to power the electrics.

❄️

Weak AC at Signals

The Aqua AC compressor is electric and runs off the hybrid battery. When the pack is weak, the AC blows warm in stop-and-go traffic and only cools once the engine is charging.

🖥️

Fault Codes Stored

A scan shows P0A80 (replace hybrid battery pack), P0A7F (battery pack deterioration) or P3000 (HV battery control system). We read these free at your door.

Which Pack?

NiMH vs Lithium for the Aqua

Both are good choices. The right one depends on how long you plan to keep the car and how you drive.

NiMH: the standard, proven option

The same chemistry Toyota fitted at the factory. It is cheaper, modules are widely available, the car's computer needs no modification, and it can be serviced module by module later. Its drawbacks are weight and a shorter life in heat. With a clean cooling filter it gives a good 5 years in Karachi, which is what our warranty covers.

Lithium: lighter, better fuel average, longer life

A lithium pack is noticeably lighter, holds its charge better in stop-and-go traffic, and lets the car run in EV mode more often. Most owners see 2 to 4 km/L more than a fresh NiMH pack in the city. It handles heat better and does not fade the way NiMH does, so we expect 7 to 8 years from it, and its built-in BMS balances the cells. The disadvantage is simple: it costs more up front, and module work later is a specialist job rather than a swap.

Our recommendation

  • Keeping the car 2–3 years, or planning to sell: NiMH. It is the lower cost and the 5-year warranty transfers with the car, which helps resale.
  • Keeping the car 5 years or more, or driving 40 km+ a day in city traffic: Lithium. The fuel saving alone pays back the difference in about two years.
  • Ride-hailing or commercial use: Lithium. The extra cycle life and heat tolerance matter most when the car runs 10–12 hours a day.
  • Budget is tight and the pack only has a couple of weak modules: ask about module repair first. We will tell you honestly if it is worth it.
