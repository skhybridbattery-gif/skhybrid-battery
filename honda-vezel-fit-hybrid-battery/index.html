Symptoms

Signs Your Vezel or Fit Hybrid Battery Is Failing

A Honda hybrid battery usually fades over months rather than dying in a day. If you notice two or more of these signs, book a free diagnostic.

⚠️

IMA or Hybrid Warning Light

On the Fit GP1 it is the orange IMA light. On the Vezel, Fit GP5 and Grace you get a "Check Hybrid System" warning, often in Japanese on imported cars. The car may stop giving electric assist.

⛽

Fuel Average Drops

When the pack cannot store energy, the petrol engine does nearly all the work. The average slides towards that of an ordinary petrol car, worst of all in stop-and-go traffic.

🔋

Battery Gauge Swings

The charge gauge drops from nearly full to a couple of bars within minutes, then refills after a little braking. Fast, large swings point to weak cells.

🔊

Engine Keeps Running at Idle

Idle-stop no longer switches the engine off at signals, or it restarts within seconds, because the battery cannot hold enough charge to run the electrics.

🐢

Jerky at Low Speed

The i-DCD system relies on the electric motor to smooth out pulling away. With a weak pack there is less electric help, so creeping in traffic can feel jerky or hesitant. A battery does not fix a gearbox fault, though.

🖥️

Fault Codes Stored

A scan may show hybrid battery codes such as P0A7F (battery pack deterioration) or P0A80 (replace hybrid battery pack). We read the codes free at your door.

Lithium or NiMH?

Which Battery Does Your Honda Have?

The most common confusion we hear. Honda changed battery technology in 2013, so it depends on your generation.

Vezel, Fit GP5/GP6 and Grace: lithium-ion (i-DCD)

These cars use Honda's i-DCD hybrid system: a 1.5-litre petrol engine, a 7-speed dual-clutch gearbox with an electric motor built in, and a compact lithium-ion battery. Honda fitted lithium from the factory, so the correct replacement is always a lithium pack. A NiMH pack is not a like-for-like fit for these cars.

Fit Hybrid GP1: NiMH (IMA)

The older Fit Hybrid uses Honda's IMA system: a 1.3-litre engine with a thin electric motor between the engine and a CVT gearbox, powered by a nickel-metal hydride (NiMH) battery. It is a completely different pack, with a different size, voltage and connectors. It is the same chemistry Toyota uses in the Aqua, which is why people assume every hybrid battery is NiMH.

How to check yours: the chassis number in your registration book starts with the code in the table, for example RU3, GP5 or GP1. Send us a photo on WhatsApp and we will confirm.

Be careful with one-price quotes: if a shop quotes one "Vezel battery" price without asking your chassis code, ask exactly which cells they will fit. Toyota owners can compare our Toyota Aqua hybrid battery page.

Causes

Why Vezel Batteries Fail in Pakistan

Lithium-ion is tougher than NiMH, but Pakistani conditions are hard on any hybrid battery.

Heat

Lithium cells handle heat better than NiMH, but still lose capacity faster when hot. A Vezel parked in the summer sun gets very hot inside, and the battery is cooled with that same cabin air.

A blocked cooling intake near the rear seat

The battery has a small fan that pulls cabin air in through an intake vent near the rear seat. That vent is often covered by seat covers, bags or a child seat, or clogged with dust. With poor airflow the cells run hotter, the car uses the battery less, and the pack ages faster.

Short trips and long parking

Many Vezels and Fits do school runs and short trips in heavy traffic, then sit parked for days. Standing for long periods with a low charge is hard on any battery; a 20 to 30 minute drive once or twice a week helps.

Age before import

Most Vezels and Fits in Pakistan were used in Japan for several years before import. The battery in a 2015 Vezel is now over ten years old, whatever the odometer says.
