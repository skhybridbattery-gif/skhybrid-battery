Symptoms

Signs Your Axio or Fielder Battery Is Failing

The pack fades over a few months, and the car tells you in these ways. If you notice two or more, book a free diagnostic.

⛽

Fuel Average Drops

When the pack weakens, the engine does all the work and the average falls close to that of a normal 1.5-litre petrol car. It shows most in city traffic, where the hybrid should save the most.

🔋

Battery Gauge Jumps Full to Empty

The gauge shows full, drops to one or two bars within a minute of driving, then climbs back while braking. Weak modules cannot hold a charge, so the reading swings.

⚠️

Hybrid System Warning / Red Triangle

"Check Hybrid System" with the red triangle and master warning light. The car may go into limp mode or refuse to start. The pack has failed a self-test.

🔊

Engine Runs Constantly at Idle

A healthy Axio switches the engine off at signals. If it keeps running or restarts every few seconds, the pack cannot hold enough charge to power the electrics.

❄️

Weak AC at Signals

Like the Aqua, the AC compressor is electric and runs off the hybrid battery. When the pack is weak, the AC blows warm in stop-and-go traffic and only cools once the engine is charging.

🖥️

Fault Codes Stored

A scan shows P0A80 (replace hybrid battery pack), P0A7F (battery pack deterioration) or P3000 (HV battery control system). We read these free at your door.

Which Pack?

NiMH vs Lithium for the Axio and Fielder

Both are good choices. The right one depends on how hard the car works and how long you will keep it.

NiMH: the standard, proven option

The same chemistry Toyota fitted at the factory. It costs less, the car's computer needs no changes, and it can be serviced module by module later. Its drawbacks are weight and a shorter life in heat.

Lithium: lighter and tougher

A lithium pack is noticeably lighter, holds its charge better in stop-and-go traffic and lets the car run on electric power more often. It handles heat and constant cycling better than NiMH, and its built-in BMS keeps the cells balanced. It costs more up front, and later module work is a specialist job.

Why the Fielder often suits lithium

Many Fielders in Pakistan work hard: family trips with a full boot, school and office runs, goods for a shop, or long ride-hailing shifts. Heavy loads and long hours push the battery through far more charge cycles in heat. That is exactly where lithium's extra durability pays off.

Our recommendation

  • Selling within 2–3 years, or light use: NiMH. Lower cost, and the 5-year warranty helps resale.
  • Keeping the car 5 years or more: lithium, especially for daily city driving.
  • Family loads, commercial or ride-hailing use: lithium. Cycle life and heat tolerance matter most here.
  • Tight budget and only a couple of weak modules: ask about module repair first.
